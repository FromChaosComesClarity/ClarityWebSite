the_library.md

One shelf, everything on it

Games arrive from six different places and stop being six different libraries.

01_importing

Six kinds of game, one shelf

  • Steam — your whole account, with playtime and achievements.
  • GOG and Epic — signed in inside the app, installed without a store client.
  • itch.io, Flatpak and anything else — if you can run it from a terminal, it can go on the shelf.
  • PICO-8 carts — hundreds of them, with their own artwork.
  • Emulated games — brought across from EmuLatte, kept in step.

Covers, hero art, logos, descriptions, genres, release years, HowLongToBeat times and review scores are fetched once and kept locally. A store changing its API does not empty your shelf.

02_the_game_page

Everything about a game, in one place

  • Manuals. GOG ships PDFs with a lot of games. They are downloaded, attached and read in place.
  • Achievements from GOG and Steam in a single list, with what you have unlocked.
  • Save games backed up to a zip and restored from one, without leaving the page.
  • Trailers, screenshots and a description worth reading.
  • Compatibility — Proton version, prefix, winetricks, environment variables, all per game.
03_your_data

The library is a folder you can carry

Everything the app knows lives in one folder beside the AppImage: the database, every cover and screenshot it fetched, your playlists, your save backups, your settings.

  • Copy it and it moves. Another machine, another drive, a USB stick — it arrives complete.
  • Back it up by copying it. There is no export step, because there is nothing to export.
  • Delete it and it is gone. Nothing is left behind anywhere else.
  • No account and no cloud. Nothing is uploaded, because there is nowhere to upload it to.
04_and_the_awkward_ones

The games that do not just work

Some games need more than a launch command. Those get handled one at a time — named games with named faults — and the things that are not games at all, like source ports and fan remakes, get their own catalogue.
